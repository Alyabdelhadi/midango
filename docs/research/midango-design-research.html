Midango design research

Research for the design direction of Midango, a court-booking platform that replaces Velocity Play. It covers the admin side (MIDANGO Business and MIDANGO Desk) and the customer side (the MIDANGO App, built in Flutter). It is for review before any design or build work starts.

Prepared 17 November 2026 · Inputs: MIDANGO_Brand_Guidelines.pdf (5 pages), Midango Motion Play Logo.png, Midango Motion Logo.png, the Velocity Play repository, and web research. This is a local document and has not been published.

The recommendation in ten lines

  1. Start with courts. Design only for booking a court in v1. Classes and events come later and use the same slot model. Velocity Play tried to cover every vertical at once, and its screens ended up general-purpose.
  2. In the customer app, open slots appear on the venue card. Tapping a time opens the confirm sheet: time → Confirm → done. The customer pays cash at the venue.
  3. Customers pick a time first and a court second. "Any available court" is the default, and choosing a specific court is optional.
  4. Confirming is a bottom sheet, not a new page. Customers pay in cash at the venue for now. The total including VAT, "Pay at the venue" and the cancellation terms are all on the sheet.
  5. Three tabs: Play, Bookings, Profile. Loyalty, streaks and feeds wait until there is a reason to add them.
  6. The admin is split into two products, using the brand guide's own names. Business is for owners and managers. Desk is for the counter.
  7. The Schedule is the home of Business. Courts are columns and time runs down the page. Click an empty cell to book, and drag a booking to move it.
  8. Every status has a word as well as a colour. Free, Pay at venue, Paid, Checked in and Blocked are written in each cell, not shown by colour alone.
  9. Lime is a fill, never text on white. At 1.2:1 against white it fails contrast. Navy text on a lime fill gives 15:1.
  10. Light surfaces are the default in both products. This follows the guide's ratio of 65–75% light surfaces. It reverses Velocity Play's dark-first app.

Part 1 · Brand

Brand decisions to approve

The guide fixes the palette, typefaces, logo configurations, voice and the three product tones: App is "bright, friendly", Business is "professional and restrained", and Desk is "fast and functional". It leaves the items below open. Each card has a proposal and a reason.

All ten approved on 17 November 2026 These are now the brand rules for design and development.

D1 · COLOR ROLES

Lime is only a fill. Navy is the text on top of it.

The guide says lime is for "calls to action, highlights, active states". On white, lime measures 1.24:1, which is unreadable as text, icons or thin borders. Proposal: use lime only as a background fill (primary buttons, selected chips, available time slots, the active tab indicator), always with Midnight Navy content on top (15.0:1). On navy surfaces, lime may be used as text (15.0:1).

This keeps lime's energy and makes every lime element readable. It also stops the "lime everywhere" problem the guide's Do/Don't page warns about.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D2 · LINKS

Add a darker cyan, "Cyan Ink" (#00788A), for links and focus rings on light surfaces.

The guide gives cyan to "links". Electric Cyan (#00CDEB) on white is 1.9:1, which fails contrast. Proposal: add a darker shade, #00788A (5.2:1 on white), for link text and focus outlines. Keep Electric Cyan #00CDEB for gradients, decoration and links on navy (9.7:1).

It stays in the same colour family the guide asks for, and links and focus become readable.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D3 · SECONDARY TEXT

Add a muted grey (#5B6478) for secondary text. Deep Slate becomes the colour for body text.

Deep Slate (#202B43) is so close to Navy (#071329) that it does not read as secondary text: their luminances are 0.024 and 0.007. Proposal: Navy for headings, Deep Slate for body text, and a new Muted #5B6478 for metadata and help text (5.9:1 on white, 5.5:1 on Cloud White).

Hierarchy needs three visible steps of text colour. The guide only provides two dark ones.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D4 · STATUS COLORS

Add status colours. The guide defines none.

Booking software needs error, warning and success states. Proposal: Error #D92D20 on #FEF0EF, Warning #B54708 on #FFF4E5, Info = Cyan Ink. Success uses lime as a fill with a ✓ and a text label, so no new green is added.

Without these, developers will improvise reds and greens per screen. Lime already means "go", so a separate green would compete with it.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D5 · TYPOGRAPHY

Space Grotesk for headings only, Inter for everything else, and a font for Arabic is still needed

The guide recommends Space Grotesk, Inter and Manrope but does not assign them to roles in the product. Proposal: Space Grotesk for screen titles, prices and booking times (it has the athletic feel). Inter for all UI text and admin tables. Drop Manrope to avoid a third family. Gap: the guide covers no Arabic. If Midango keeps Velocity Play's English + Arabic support, add IBM Plex Sans Arabic, which was already used in Velocity Play and pairs well with Inter.

Two families keep the app light. A display face on prices is what makes a booking app feel branded rather than generic.

Decided Arabic: yes (Q1). Use IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic text, with Space Grotesk and Inter for Latin text, and full right-to-left mirroring.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D6 · LOGO PLACEMENT

Which logo goes where

See Logo placement. Proposal in short: the M icon alone in app bars, the admin sidebar and the app icon. The secondary logo (no tagline) on sign-in and splash screens. The full logo with tagline only on the website and in marketing.

The guide says to use the full logo only "when the tagline remains comfortably readable", which rules it out inside the product UI.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D7 · THEME

Light theme by default in the app and in Business. Dark theme is optional and comes later.

The guide calls for 65–75% white or light surfaces. Proposal: ship light first. Use navy for the Desk's top bar and the app's booking-confirmation screen, where it adds emphasis. Add dark mode in v1.1 as a token swap.

Velocity Play was dark-first. That clashed with the new guide and doubled the QA work.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D8 · GRADIENT

The lime-to-cyan gradient appears in three places only: splash, booking confirmation and marketing.

The guide says "in small doses". Limiting it to these places keeps it special and keeps it off controls.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D9 · ICONS

Use one outlined icon set: Lucide on the web (React), and the same glyphs in Flutter via lucide_icons.

The guide asks for "a consistent family of simple outlined icons" and "do not mix". One set across React and Flutter keeps the two products consistent.

Approved 17 Nov 2026

D10 · SCOPE vs BRAND

The guide positions Midango for "courts, classes, events and experiences". Your brief says it is a court-booking platform.

Proposal: design v1 for courts only, but keep the copy general ("Find your game", not "Book a padel court"), so classes can be added later without rewriting it.

Approved 17 Nov 2026 Courts only for v1, with general copy.

Part 2 · Admin: MIDANGO Business & Desk

Admin & courts dashboard

The brand guide already separates the two jobs, and the design should follow it. Business (owner or manager: set up courts and prices, see money and occupancy) and Desk (counter staff: who is here now, check them in, take a walk-in) are the same React app with two layouts, chosen by role.

Velocity Play's admin put both jobs into one generic CRUD back office. Counter staff need three actions. Owners need twenty.

Navigation model: MIDANGO Business

Left sidebar, 7 items, with task names rather than database names

  1. Today: the home screen
  2. Schedule: the court grid
  3. Bookings: list and search
  4. Customers
  5. Courts & prices
  6. Reports
  7. Settings: venue, staff, cash and policies

A venue switcher sits at the top for multi-venue owners. A global + New booking button and a search box are always visible in the header. Search finds bookings by customer name, booking reference or the last 4 digits of the phone number. Full phone numbers are never shown (Q2).

Platform operators (Midango staff, not venues) get one extra item: Sport categories.

Seven items fit without scrolling, which is about the limit for non-technical staff. Names like "Courts & prices" describe a job. Velocity Play showed data-model words such as "Resources", "Pricing Rules", "Schedules" and "Activities" Observed.

Each of CourtReserve and Playtomic Manager puts the scheduler at the centre. Playtomic calls the Schedule "the heart of Playtomic Manager" Verified.

A search box that is always present answers the most common phone call, "do I have a booking?", from any screen.

MIDANGO Desk (counter mode)

Layout: a single screen with no sidebar, built for a tablet or laptop at the counter. A navy top bar with the M icon and "Desk", the clock and a search box.

  • Left (60%): a "Now & next" list in time order. Each row: court, time, name, the cash amount due and one large button: Take cash & check in (one tap does both), or Check in if already paid, then ✓ Done.
  • After 15 minutes without a check-in, the row offers Mark no-show.
  • Right (40%): the court columns for the next 3 hours, read-only, with "+ Walk-in" on free cells.
  • Scan the customer's QR code to jump straight to their booking.
  • An undo bar for 10 seconds after a check-in or payment.

The guide describes Desk as "fast and functional. Prioritize check-in, today's schedule and quick booking management."

One button per row, decided by the booking's state, means no menus while a customer waits at the counter. With cash only (Q4), taking the money and checking in happen together, so they share one tap.

Undo is safer than an "Are you sure?" dialog for frequent actions. Velocity Play had to add undo for check-in after the fact Observed.

Navy chrome tells staff which mode they are in, and it is the only place outside the app where navy is a large surface.

Part 3 · Customer app: MIDANGO App (Flutter)

Customer booking flow

The aim is the fewest steps from "I want to play tonight" to a paid booking. Most competitors use the order venue → date → court → time → book → pay. Padel Centar's listing describes exactly that order Verified. Midango should reverse it: time first, court optional, confirm in a sheet. Payment is cash at the venue for now (Q3, Q4), which removes the payment step from the app entirely.

Shortest path (returning, signed-in user)

Open appPlay tab, tonight near you→ Tap 1a time chip on a venue card (e.g. 20:00)→ Confirm sheetduration 90 min preselected, any court, price incl. VAT, "Pay at the venue"→ Tap 2Confirm booking→ Donethe sheet turns into the confirmation

Two taps after opening the app. Open time slots appear on the result cards, so the list of results is already a slot picker.

Full path (new user or wants details)

Searchsport · date · time window→ Venuephotos, open times by date→ Timetap a slot (+ optional court)→ Sign inphone OTP, only if a guest→ Confirm sheetreview & confirm→ Confirmation

Guests can browse everything, and sign-in only appears at payment. Velocity Play's spec already had this rule, and it should be kept. There is no onboarding carousel and no profile setup before the first booking.

Booking management (Bookings tab)

  • Next booking at the top as a large card with a countdown ("in 2 h 15 min") and actions: Directions, Share, QR.
  • Every upcoming booking shows the amount to bring: "Pay SAR 180 at the venue".
  • Upcoming list, then a Past list with a Book again button that opens the confirm sheet at the same venue, court type and duration on the next matching day.
  • Cancel: nothing has been paid, so there is no refund. The sheet says plainly what cancelling means: "Free to cancel until Tue 14:00", or after that time, "Late cancellation: this counts toward your no-show limit".
  • Change time: v1.1, shown as "Cancel and rebook" when the policy allows it.

Questions like "when is it, where, how much do I bring, and what if I can't go" should be answered on one card. Telling people the consequence before they cancel is the most effective trust measure in this flow.

Flutter implementation notes for the handoff

  • Put the tokens in a ThemeExtension (colours, radii, spacing) built from this document. Use ColorScheme with primary = lime and onPrimary = navy, and never use lime as a foreground on surfaces.
  • Confirm sheet as a showModalBottomSheet with DraggableScrollableSheet, so the venue stays visible behind it.
  • Sport chips, icons and court-feature filters are built from the categories API. Map each category's icon key to the shared icon set, and don't ship sport names in the app.
  • Slot chips: Wrap of custom chips with tap targets of at least 44 × 44, and Semantics labels such as "20:00, available, 180 riyals".
  • Arabic is confirmed (Q1): use Directionality and EdgeInsetsDirectional everywhere, render times in the locale's digits, and test every screen in both directions. Don't mirror the logo.
  • No payment SDK in v1. Keep the payment method as a value from the API ("cash_at_venue"), so online methods can be added later without changing the UI.

Replacing Velocity Play

How Midango should differ from a typical sports booking app

Typical appMidangoWhy
Venue → court → date → time → book → pay (5–6 screens)Time chip on the card → confirm sheet (2 taps), pay cash at the venueThe biggest saving available, and the clearest reason to switch apps.
Players pick the court first"Any available court" by defaultMost players care about the time. Matching shows more open times.
Fees appear at the last stepThe total including VAT is on every Pay button and on the slot chipsMatches the brand guide's "payment clarity and confidence".
A social or feed-heavy home screenHome is the searchApp Store reviews of Playtomic complain it is "too much going on".
One admin for everyoneBusiness (manage) and Desk (operate)The brand guide already names these two products.
Custom colours per booking type in adminFixed colours per state, plus textReadable by any staff member at any venue, including people with colour blindness.
